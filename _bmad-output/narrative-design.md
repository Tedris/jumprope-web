## Story Foundation

### Narrative Premise

A nobody has been quietly training — rope, stride, lift, read — for hours that mean nothing yet. When portals suddenly overrun the world, the montage reveals itself as chapter one's tutorial: the System that's been logging their days has been *preparing* them all along. Failing forward through days they can never lose, they become the hero the news already mentioned by name.

*Stakes note:* the lossless law replaces failure-stakes with **relevance-stakes** — the world resolves its gates without you (spawn vs clear, felt as TV tone); you can never lose anything, but you can miss everything.

### Core Themes

1. **Growth requires failure** — supercompensation as philosophy; the mechanics make fear useless — every trip was fertilizer. What you avoid is the only thing that truly stops you.
2. **The past as company** — shadows, diary, worn room: you live among your own yesterdays, and the game's melancholy-comic heart is that they are content, and you are not yet done.
3. **The unspectacular hours of a hero** — an ode to the montage; the genre's chapter one you never got to play, delivered as the whole game before you notice.
4. **Self-making as a system** — the body as an authored stat sheet; the System reads as a mirror, not a master: its panels are your diary's tallies renamed, its voice is yours retinted.

### Tone and Atmosphere

**Tone:** Melancholy-comic register (locked, GDD Art Style) — serious and comedic *simultaneously*; the joke channel that stops for the news is the register's signature beat. Intimate room, epic radio (the world arrives as broadcast, never as walkable place). Hopeful with a sad undertow; act two injects genuine menace — the ice-water first hit — without breaking the register.

**Atmosphere:** PS1 lo-fi warmth (dither, wobble, procedural voices); room-wear as memory made visible; TV murmur as the sound of a world you don't live in yet; late game = many quiet choreographies running at once, yours.

**Emotional register:** cozy competence → curious unease → cold-water awe → curator's pride, faintly elegiac.

## Story Structure

### Structure Type

**3-Act**, on the brainstorm's act skeleton; the genre mask peels rhythm → life sim → raising sim → hero sim (shadow thread adds memory sim).

### Act Breakdown

- **Act I — Quiet Training (the montage disguised as the whole game).** Pure rope-game life; day grammar; caps, compost, diary. Zero narrative UI, zero lore — only worn room, diary lines, infomercials. Length: many hours, deliberately uneventful; the longest act per player. Ends once — the weather report is interrupted (Act II opener is an event in the world, not in the UI).
- **Act II — The News & the Awakening.** The world intrudes: gates on the corkboard, RANK/LEVEL debut in-world, the stat panel renames itself from the diary's tallies. First dungeon = the first hurt (ice-water beat, emotion pass owed). The awakening: past days become shadows. The player learns the System was here all along.
- **Act III — Hero Sim (resolution as a state, not an event).** Board management against the hunter economy; the curated room montage; the player's name on the chyron = the completion statement. After: designed endless — room, shadows, board keep playing; the diary→chyron loop closes (diary lines quoted back as news).

## Story Beats

### Major Story Beats

1. **Day One** — rope only; the diary opens mid-sentence. Systems are the story. *(emergent)*
2. **First Cap** — the income line flattens mid-day; restraint taught wordlessly. *(emergent)*
3. **First Failure Day** — the trip arrives on schedule; tomorrow's diary line stands dotted, one notch up. Supercompensation becomes hope. *(emergent)*
4. **Station Arrivals** — thresholds crossed, a vignette: "okay, I can also do this." *(emergent)*
5. **First Life Card** — the first permanent self-edit at an earned milestone. *(emergent)*
6. **The Interrupt** — mid-weather-report, the impossible news: portals. Trigger: deterministic day ~15–20 (balance-bound) — the world keeps its own schedule. *(scripted; Act II opener)*
7. **The Panel Rename** — the diary's mundane tallies re-title into System stat lines. First and only UI "reveal." *(scripted, diegetic)*
8. **The Board** — gates appear on the corkboard; the TV quotes an "E-rank" — the first hidden number with a name. *(systems)*
9. **The First Hurt** — through the impossible door, the first real hit after hours of safety. Ice water. *(emotion pass owed)*
10. **LV 1** — stepping through a gate, a number appears on nothing. *(in-world debut)*
11. **The Awakening** — returning from the **first dungeon clear**, a past day is standing in the corner, jumping rope. The split power cracks open through the fight-memory. *(scripted)*
12. **First Shadow Posted** — yesterday fights a gate for you; manual→automatic, per gate. *(emergent)*
13. **Ejection** — you lose a dungeon; the gate politely throws you out; tomorrow it resets. Losing taught politeness. *(emergent)*
14. **The Dread Arithmetic** — spawn rate vs clearing rate, told entirely as TV tone; relevance-stakes, no timer UI. *(systems)*
15. **The Name** — the chyron carries your name; later, your own diary lines quoted back. **Completion statement.** *(scripted apex)*
16. **The Curated Room** — the montage runs live in its corners; designed endless. *(state)*

### Beat Placement by Act

**Act I — Quiet Training:** beats 1–5 (all emergent; zero scripted lore) → ends on beat 6.
**Act II — The News & the Awakening:** beats 6–11, 13 — scripted beats land through diegetic objects only; the player's first dangers and first memories outsourced.
**Act III — Hero Sim:** beats 12, 14–16 — systems narrate; The Name resolves; the Curated Room is the state after resolution; optional S-rank apex for chasers, never mandatory.

## Pacing and Flow

### Narrative Tempo

Slow burn. Act I deliberately uneventful (the longest act, the disguise at work); act II shocks re-read act I retroactively; act III warm denouement that keeps playing.

### Tension Curve

Flat plateau punctuated by shocks — **not waves**: the Interrupt and the First Hurt are the two spikes; everything else is either cozy competence or systems-pressure (caps, plateaus, shelf life) which is tension without alarm.

### Story Density

Near-zero in act I (world exists only as wear, diary, mumble) → medium in act II (events only through corkboard/TV/door — zero new UI) → high in act III (the news carries your story back to you).

### Key Moments

**Highest tension:** The First Hurt (beat 9). **Emotional climax:** The Awakening (beat 11) — past and present occupy one room. **Resolution beat:** The Name (beat 15); the Curated Room is its afterimage, by design.

## Characters

### Protagonist

#### The Skipper (named once, in the diary — day 1)

**Description:** A nobody with a jump rope and a barbell. Blocky PS1 chibi, scarf chain, sweat geometry; never speaks a line, only writes them.

**Background:** The Alley. No origin tragedy — origin tragedy is what this genre does to people; this game declines the trope. Where he comes from is the room; the room is the biography.

**Motivation:** Unstated by design ("just getting stronger") — a deliberate negative space the reveal fills retroactively: he was being *prepared*; the hours had a recipient.

**Strengths:** Discipline without ceiling; patience the player performs rather than reads; a funny stubbornness about mornings.

**Flaws:** Can't sit still; measures care in reps; slow to notice the world happening (the player is never faster than the news).

**Conflicts:**

- Internal: **more vs. enough** — the room says it's enough; tomorrow says more. Resolved not by winning but by re-framing: "more" turns out to have been "ready" all along.
- External: a world resolving its own gates without him — relevance, not survival, is the pressure.

---

### Antagonist

**None — by doctrine.** The counterforce is **the world's indifference**: the hunter economy clears gates whether or not the player shows up; spawn vs. clear arithmetic is the only "threat," told as TV tone. Monsters are weather, not malice — real danger, no villain. The **System** is a mirror, not a master: its panels are his tallies, its voice is his diary; antagonizing it would betray theme 4.

---

### Supporting Cast (functional, diegetic)

#### The Diary — mentor/informant, later the System

First-person, flat, faintly funnier each act ("day 9: failed on the rope. legs feel stupid. tomorrow more."). The tutorial voice that grows up into the System panel — the same handwriting, unsettling on purpose. Voice rule: never explains a mechanic; states conclusions the player already lived.

#### The TV — chorus

Infomercials ("PROGRAM SEVEN: TO FAILURE!") → weather/sports → news → the player's name. The joke channel that stops for the news is its one moment of drama; otherwise it reports, like weather, that the world is moving.

#### The Shadows — silent foils

Frozen past-days, content, never knowing it's a loop. They ask nothing; they *are* the question. Deliberate **anti-arc**: they never grow — growth is the protagonist's burden by contract, and the room's argument for more-vs-enough made furniture.

#### The Other Hunters — crowd, never cast

Unnamed, unseen, statistically real: the board's tick and the news' subject. They give the world competence without casting a single rival.

## Character Arcs

### The Skipper — Environmental Arc (flat hero, moving world)

**Starting state:** A person and a rope; meaning absent, discipline present.
**Transformation moments:** he doesn't change — the *facts about him* do: the Interrupt (the world needs doing), the Panel Rename (his log was a System), the Awakening (his past is company), The Name (his hours were a story others tell).
**Ending state:** the same man, louder world — a hero the news can name, still jumping rope at dawn because that part was never means-to-an-end.
**Lessons learned:** by the player, not the character — growth requires failure; the past is company; the unspectacular hours were the plot.

### The Shadows — Anti-arc (the changeless)

Content repetition as mirror; their stillness measures the player's motion. Retire/re-cast is the only "character work" — and it is the *player's* arc performed on them.

**Cast summary:** 1 protagonist · 0 antagonists (counterforce: world-indifference) · 4 functional voices.

## World Building

### World Overview

**Setting:** One vertical city — the v2 ascent map as geography: **Alley** (the gym's street) → Rooftops → Blimp Deck → Stratosphere → Moon Approach. Analog-modern, deliberately un-updated: local TV, infomercials, corkboard economies, payphone-era texture. The PS1 register *is* the era's memory of itself. The game begins in a ground-floor gym with one window.

**World Type:** Contemporary with late-arriving portal fantasy — the genre arrives *on schedule, mid-game*, as news.

**World Rules:**

- **Gates** appeared the week of the Interrupt (world-wide, simultaneous, origin unexplained — weather, not malice). They persist until cleared; contents respawn; they eject the losing — the emergency-extract convention is public knowledge, which is why nobody fears them the way old stories feared dungeons.
- **Ranks** are reputations, not memberships: E→S computed off public results (the in-world twin of the hidden RANK). Anyone can clear; nobody must.
- **The System's scope:** it logged *everyone* who trained; a preparation program with no committee. The comic-cosmic rule: **his hobby happened to be the meta build.**

**Atmosphere:** mundane competence; municipal uncanny (dungeons like weather fronts moving through neighborhoods); the world's loudest fact happening politely *outside* the one room that stays yours.

**Unique Elements:** a portal-fantasy world experienced almost entirely as *broadcast and real estate*; the gym is the only persistent room either genre's canon has ever given you — the raid hub that happens to be a training room.

---

### History and Backstory

**Timeline Overview:**

- **Before:** an ordinary city; one nobody's morning routine. Deliberately no deeper history — the absence *is* the disguise.
- **The week of the Interrupt:** portals world-wide, simultaneously; the news can't decide if it's weather or war; the gym's TV decides: both, in a chyron.
- **The adaptation months (act II–III, lived by the player):** gig boards formalize gates; rankings trend like sports; the hunter economy finds its tick rate — the city outsources its own apocalypse to piecework.
- **Now, ongoing:** the ordinary and the impossible coexist with municipal politeness. Nobody explains the System. It files.

**Legends and Myths:** the genre lore the characters grew up on (chosen ones, dungeons, ranks) — played as *inaccurate folklore*: no chosen one, no association, no final boss; just logistics and one man's mornings.

**Hidden Secrets:** whether the System prepares anyone else — never answered; the news never mentions a second "program." The silence reads as exclusivity or indifference, by the player's taste.

---

### Factions and Organizations

**None formal — economies, not clubs.** The **board** (gate listings, public, first-come), the **rank census** (E–S as public perception), the **networks** (TV as aggregator). Relationships are supply/demand. Story role: give the world competence, motion, and indifference without a single committee scene.

---

### Key Locations

#### The Gym (the room → the rooms)

**Description:** Concrete, dithered, warm; corners as stations; wear as biography; grows study, weights bay, the door. **Significance:** the persistent present tense — the only "level" that changes with the player, not the plot. **Atmosphere:** safe before it is safe. **Events:** beats 1–5, 11, 16. **Inhabitants:** him, then his yesterdays.

#### The Corkboard

**Description:** Pinned slips; INT writes more of them. **Significance:** the world map as furniture; shelf life as plot. **Events:** 8, 13, 14.

#### The TV

**Description:** The fourth channel of voice. **Significance:** the world's window and the mask's slip channel. **Events:** 6, 15 — and every day's murmur.

#### The Impossible Door / Dungeons

**Description:** The gym's own space, palette-flipped and fogged — *this room, wrong*. **Significance:** danger without geography; the dungeon is the domestic minus the warm color. **Events:** 9, 10, 13.

#### The City (seen, never walked)

**Description:** The ascent view from window and news — the Alley he skips in; rooftops the rope-day climbs reach. **Significance:** the unreachable epic that keeps the intimate room the only present tense; rope days touch it one ceiling at a time.

## Dialogue Framework

### Dialogue Style

**Overall Voice:** No conversations exist — *voice* arrives as systems writing about you. The zero-tutorial-copy rule governs everything: no voice explains a mechanic; voices comment on what the player already lived.

**Style Elements:**

- Formality: diary casual → System flat-casual (one lineage, one gear shift)
- Period: analog-modern — infomercial cadence, sports-ticker shorthand, no internet idiom
- Verbosity: concise to the point of terse; nightfall diary lines target one sentence, always under three
- Humor: deadpan-with-warmth; the joke is never the player's failure, always the System's / the world's tone
- Profanity: none (all-ages, PS1-broadcast register)

**Voice Distinctions:**

| Voice | Register | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Diary (him)** | blunt → fluent as stats rise — *prose is a stat*: STR economy, VIT warmth, AGI rhythm, INT wit | never explains; states conclusions; day 1: "legs feel stupid. tomorrow more." |
| **System** | the diary's voice in a machine's clothes (retint, same lineage — unsettling on purpose) | reactive (caps, ejections, castings), never initiates small talk; quality scales with INT |
| **TV** | anchor-neutral; sports-ticker for ranks, weather-calm for apocalypse | reports; never addresses the player |
| **Card text** | the game's own voice, pun-first, structurally-homage-safe (no Scarlet Skips wording, ever) | names mechanics by feel, never by numbers (numbers live on the card body) |
| **Announce voice** | sports-ticker: "LOCAL SKIPPER, E-RANK" | numbers only, no adjectives |

---

### Key Voiced Moments

1. **The Naming** (day 1) — the diary's only question: one word. Purpose: contract + the chyron's future material.
2. **Nightfall summaries** (daily) — diary line + sparse System line; the rhythm the whole game listens to.
3. **Milestone cards** — the voice's set pieces: life-card text lands the event's meaning.
4. **The Interrupt bulletin** — the first non-mundane TV text; weather anchor mid-sentence → the chyron. Tone: broadcast trying to stay broadcast.
5. **The Rename** — the System's first sentence, in the diary's handwriting. The voice reveal, not a cutscene.
6. **First Hurt / First Clear / Ejection** — TV + System one-liners; the world noting him without noting him.
7. **The Name chyron** — the payoff string; then diary→chyron quotes (his lines, read back, unattributed).

Purpose rule: every voiced moment lands through an object (page, screen, board) — never a face, never a subtitle bar.

---

### Branching Dialogue System

**None — conditional pools, not trees.** Every line is state-keyed (act, stat tiers, INT rows, milestone flags, shadow roster) and *deterministic*: the same state reads the same words — the determinism contract that governs shadow replays governs prose. No player-text choice exists in fiction, so no branch state exists; choices live in the mechanics, where they belong. Content = data tables (mods-friendly, zero code), versioned with the save.

## Environmental Storytelling

### Visual Storytelling

**Set Dressing:** The room-wear ledger *is* the biography — AGI groove worn in the floorboard, bent barbell, diary towers by the corner, scuffed sprint-wall. Nothing decorative: every prop is a receipt. The room growing rooms (study, weights bay, the door) is the only "level art progression" the story needs.

**Environmental Details:** wear accumulates monotonically (the lossless law, rendered); shadows are living murals of past days — the poster shot is the room's final set dressing; the dungeon is this room's geometry palette-flipped and fogged: *environmental storytelling aimed backwards at the familiar*.

**Visual Symbolism:** the rope (constancy — the one object every act re-semantizes without touching); the window (the epic, unreachable, eventually named after you); dotted lines (tomorrow's prophecy, the visual of hope-as-mathematics); corners (the past, kept at the edges until the middle of the room is memory).

**Color and Lighting:** act I warm-sodium domestic; **the Interrupt does not repaint the room** — it adds fog at the window and one degree of cool in the TV spill; dungeons are the same palette inverted; act III returns warm, now thick with wear. Doctrine: the story changes what the room *means*, never what it *is* — re-semantization is also a color policy.

---

### Audio Storytelling

**Ambient Design:** room tone + city hum through glass; the TV murmur as the world's presence-at-a-distance (its content ladder is environmental narration: jingles set the register, weather/sports mark the ordinary's duration, the news breaks it); nightfall's page-turn as the daily metronome; the sore-walk footfalls certifying growth in the body, not the HUD.

**Music Integration:** `track.ogg` stays what it shipped as — 97 BPM, zero rhythmic relation to the rope (pillar 2). Music marks *layers*, never beats; the dungeon danger bed is absence-with-low-tone.

**Voice Beyond Dialogue:** the announce voice as stadium texture; the System's single retinted sentence (the Rename) as the game's only "voice acting."

**Sound Design Narrative:** **silence is the register's instrument** — the joke channel's first stop for the news; the dungeon's held breath after the first hit; the room at endgame: many quiet choreographies, one rope.

---

### Found Documents

**Document Types:** the **corkboard slips** — state-generated one-liners from real gate data (tier, comp, reward, shelf life) in fixed voice templates; INT unlocks template *rows*, so better intel is literally more sentences. The **diary** is the auto-collected found document *you authored* — re-readable anytime; the game's only archive is your own history.

**Quantity:** infinite, bounded by gates alive; zero hand-authored exceptions (template rows are content; slips are data).

**Content Focus:** world-state, not backstory — there is no backstory to find; the world has no secrets buried, only events passing.

**Discovery:** slips optional (browse is a verb); diary always available.

**Rewards:** information and shelf-life urgency only — no collectibles, no lore-achievements; the save is the brag surface.

## Narrative Delivery

### Cutscenes

**Quantity: zero — by doctrine.** No camera seizure, no face, no subtitle bar.
**The one authorized exception — the Interrupt push-in:** rope-keeping stays live (control never lost); the TV audio shifts mid-report; the camera drifts toward it — the game's *only* automatic camera move — then releases; the chyron lands while you're still jumping. First play non-skippable; replays skippable.
**Major "cutscenes":** the Rename (panel labels morph live, diary-handwriting → System sans — a UI animation, not a scene) and The Name chyron (a broadcast, not a card). Both are state events wearing diegesis.

---

### In-Game Storytelling

**Primary Methods:** objects (diary, TV, corkboard, door), systems-as-voice (state-keyed pools, deterministic), the body (sore-walk, wear geometry), camera (push-ins; otherwise never).
**Show vs. Tell:** ~95/5 — the tells are voice-pool lines about things already shown; no voice ever precedes the experience it describes.
**Interruption Approach:** zero gameplay stops, ever — even the hinge keeps the rope live. The story interrupts the *world's program*, never the player's.
**Player Control:** the diary is the archive and replay surface (every voiced moment retrievable); scripted beats replay-skippable; no text speed setting because no text gates progress.

---

### Optional Content

- **S-rank gates** — apex raid content for chasers, never required by the reveal (climax = state).
- **Rope days** — the v2 ascent interlude kept as occasional pure-climbing days: the old game living inside the new one, one ceiling at a time; the nostalgia channel for the player who learned v2.
- **Skins** — the player-authored narrative layer: costumes as *worn* identity (the shadows replay them — a past day in a top hat forever); no stat text, no dialogue ever.
- **Post-completion endless** — designed endless: room, shadows, board keep playing after The Name; the diary→chyron loop closes on itself.

---

### Ending Structure

**Single ending — and it's a state, not a screen.** No victory ceremony: the run-as-episode died with act I (life is continuous days; nothing "ends" — the lossless law's final form). The chyron (The Name) is the closest thing to an ending the game allows: a fact about you, reported while you train. Everything after is the Curated Room — resolution as continuing tense.

---

## Gameplay Integration

### Narrative-Gameplay Connection

**Integration Approach:** one sim, two readings. Every story event is a *re-semantization* of a mechanical fact — the panel was always the stats; the shadows were always the log; the reveal adds zero mechanics, only meaning. The reverse holds too: no narrative promises a mechanic the sim doesn't run (no hidden hero system; the System's power is exactly the day-scheduler the engine already had).
**Mechanic-Theme Alignment:** supercompensation → *growth requires failure*; shadows → *the past is company*; deterministic content pools → *you are not special, the world is* (until the census says otherwise — the same determinism that flattens you names you); lossless accumulation → *nothing is wasted*, the room's walls as ledger.
**Story-Gameplay Balance:** ~100% gameplay — there are no story sections; story is a *tone channel* on gameplay, densifying late.
**Ludonarrative Considerations:** pre-answered risks: **pillar 1 vs. five verbs** — corners are low-interaction rituals (click, watch); the button stays the rope's heartbeat; the one-beat rule dies only as *literal* meter, surviving as the rope's inviolability. **Flattening vs. naming** — the census trigger keeps the hero arc numeric: the news names you because the arithmetic said so, which *is* the parody and the pathos in one mechanism. **Shadow contentment vs. growth** — their stillness is the counter-argument the room must keep answering; more-vs-enough stays live forever, which is why endless exists.

---

### Story Gating

**Gating Approach:** emergent-with-dates — story gates gameplay three times, all mechanical:

- **The Interrupt** (day ~15–20, balance-bound): gates the *existence* of gates — before it, no dungeons; the one deterministic date in the fiction.
- **The Awakening** (first dungeon clear): gates shadows — posting days requires having lost something somewhere first.
- **The Board** (post-Interrupt adaptation days): gate listings appear; INT prices their intel.

**Mandatory beats:** 6, 7, 10, 11 — the four reveals: unavoidable, unskippable-first-time, diegetic.
**Optional narrative:** rope days, S-rank gates, skins, the entire post-Name endless. No area is story-locked; the city was never entered anyway.

---

### Player Agency

**Agency Level:** **witness with authored texture** — the plot has no branches; the *story* does.
**Player Influence:** the name (one word the news will say); life-card picks (permanent self-edits, mechanically load-bearing, narratively quoted back); shadow curation (which yesterdays stand in the corners — literally casting the cast); build (the stats write the diary's prose and the slips' depth).
**Choice System:** no dialogue choices by design — every choice is a mechanic wearing fiction; consequence scope = the save file, which is the whole story surface.
**Role-Playing Freedom:** the genre's power fantasy is invertible by play: the min-maxer gets the satire (logistics hero), the casual gets the cozy (a nice man's mornings) — same sim, different reading; the game never corrects either.

## Production Planning

### Writing Scope

**Estimated Word Count:** ~18,000 words total — *heavy narrative surface, small written corpus*: the story is delivered by systems, so bulk lives in pools, not scripts.

**Content Breakdown:**

- Diary pools (daily lines × stat-tier prose variants): ~3,200
- TV pools (infomercials → weather/sports → news ladder): ~6,000
- Milestone/life-card + v3 card text: ~3,800
- System lines (reactive: caps, ejections, castings, milestones): ~1,500
- Corkboard slip templates (INT-gated rows): ~1,000
- Scripted moments (naming, Interrupt bulletin, Rename sentence, chyron): ~750
- UI/system text deltas (labels, board, door): ~2,000

**Scene Count:** 6 scripted moments (naming, Interrupt, Rename, Awakening sting, ejection grammar, chyron) — everything else is emergent with a voice channel.
**Line Count:** ~1,200 state-keyed lines; all content ships as data tables (mods seam; zero copy in code — the NFR16 convention extended).
**Branching Complexity:** none — pools multiply by **state**, not by path; QA surface = state-key coverage, made tractable by the determinism contract (seeded state sweeps test prose like physics).

---

### Localization

**Approach:** English-only (status quo). Future consideration: prose pools are data tables keyed by state — a translation pass = new table columns; the scripted 750 words translate first, the pools whenever. UI has no static-text pressure (labels are few and short). No cultural-content landmines: the parody targets genre conventions, not places.

---

### Voice Acting

**Approach:** Text-only, zero recorded speech — status quo as doctrine. "Voices" remain *registers of text* (diary / System / TV / ticker) plus procedural WebAudio texture (page-turn, jingle tones, murmur). The world being watchable **with sound off and readable with type small** is an accessibility feature, not a limitation. Rationale: static-file zero-asset-debt ethos; the one sentence worth hearing (the Rename) lands harder as typography that *sounds* like a voice than as a voice.
